    Section Problem Set 8

    This problem set consists not only of problems similar to what you’ve seen, but also of unique problems you may not have seen before. The purpose is for you to apply the concepts you’ve previously learned to new, unfamiliar, and usually more interesting situations. In some cases, problems connect ideas from multiple learning objectives.
    🔗
    Write full, clear solutions to the problems below. Although the final answer is important, being able to convey you understand the underlying concepts is more important. So show your work and explain your steps. Write complete sentences in English to connect your mathematical statements. Do not use the three-dots notation ( \(\therefore\) or \(\because\) ) when you mean “therefore” or “because”. Do not use an arrow ( \(\to\) ) when you mean equals (=) or “this implies” or “and now the next step is…”. Use the worked examples in the textbook, or on solution sets to worksheets or previous problem sets, as a guide for how to write your solutions.
    🔗
    Written work should be neat and legible. Other than being written in longhand, your work should be as clean as something you would turn in for one of your writing classes. Write in linear order from left to right and top to bottom. Leave ample space for graders to write their comments. (Do not upload the exercise sheet with your answers written in the margins.) Setting aside one page per exercise is a good rule of thumb.
    🔗
    Prepare a single PDF and submit it to Gradescope. Mark the page of each exercise when you upload your pset. Make sure each page is in the proper orientation.
    🔗
    When you submit to Gradescope, you will see one extra item in the outline called “Communication & Mathematical Writing (select ALL pages)”. This is not a separate problem — it is a single, holistic score for how clearly you explain your work across your entire submission, following the writing guidelines above. When Gradescope asks you to match pages to this item, select every page of your PDF.

    5.

    A tank initially contains \(w\) L of a solution in which is dissolved \(A_0\) grams of chemical. A solution containing \(k\) g⁄L of this chemical flows into the tank at a rate of \(r\) L⁄min, and the mixture flows out at the same rate.
    🔗
    Let \(A(t)\) be the mass of chemical in the tank at time \(t\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    Find a differential equation and initial value problem satisfied by \(A(t)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Solve this initial value problem for \(A(t)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Show that as \(t\to\infty\text{,}\) the concentration of chemical in the tank approaches \(k\) g⁄L. Is this result reasonable? Explain.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    6.

    A tissue culture grows until it has a maximum area of \(M\)cm2. The area \(A(t)\) of the culture at time \(t\) may be modeled by the differential equation
    \begin{equation*} \frac{dA}{dt} = k \sqrt{A} \left(1 - \frac{A}{M}\right) \end{equation*}
    where \(k\) is a growth constant.
    🔗

    (a)

    Let \(u = \sqrt{A}\text{.}\) Show that
    \begin{equation*} \frac{du}{dt} = \frac{k}{2}\left(1- \frac{u^2}{M}\right) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Use separation of variables to find the general solution for \(u(t)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Finally, find the general solution for \(A(t)\text{.}\)
