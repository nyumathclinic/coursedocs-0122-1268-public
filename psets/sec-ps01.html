    Section Problem Set 1

    This problem set consists not only of problems similar to what you’ve seen, but also of unique problems you may not have seen before. The purpose is for you to apply the concepts you’ve previously learned to new, unfamiliar, and usually more interesting situations. In some cases, problems connect ideas from multiple learning objectives.
    🔗
    Write full, clear solutions to the problems below. Although the final answer is important, being able to convey you understand the underlying concepts is more important. So show your work and explain your steps. Write complete sentences in English to connect your mathematical statements. Do not use the three-dots notation ( \(\therefore\) or \(\because\) ) when you mean “therefore” or “because”. Do not use an arrow ( \(\to\) ) when you mean equals (=) or “this implies” or “and now the next step is…”. Use the worked examples in the textbook, or on solution sets to worksheets or previous problem sets, as a guide for how to write your solutions.
    🔗
    Written work should be neat and legible. Other than being written in longhand, your work should be as clean as something you would turn in for one of your writing classes. Write in linear order from left to right and top to bottom. Leave ample space for graders to write their comments. (Do not upload the exercise sheet with your answers written in the margins.) Setting aside one page per exercise is a good rule of thumb.
    🔗
    Prepare a single PDF and submit it to Gradescope. Mark the page of each exercise when you upload your pset. Make sure each page is in the proper orientation.
    🔗
    When you submit to Gradescope, you will see one extra item in the outline called “Communication & Mathematical Writing (select ALL pages)”. This is not a separate problem — it is a single, holistic score for how clearly you explain your work across your entire submission, following the writing guidelines above. When Gradescope asks you to match pages to this item, select every page of your PDF.

    Exercises Exercises

    1.

    Avery is measuring the volume of water in their backyard on the day of a rainstorm.
    1. Let \(T(h)\) be the total amount of rainwater in Avery’s backyard, in cubic feet, \(h\) hours after 12:00 a.m.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Let \(A(h)\) be the rate at which the rain adds water to Avery’s backyard, in cubic feet per hour, \(h\) hours after 12:00 a.m.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Let \(M(h)\) be the rate at which water drains or evaporates from Avery’s backyard, in cubic feet per hour, \(h\) hours after 12:00 a.m.
      🔗
      🔗
    The functions \(T(h)\text{,}\) \(A(h)\text{,}\) and \(M(h)\) are all differentiable functions. Assume there is no water in Avery’s backyard at 12:00 a.m.
    🔗

    (a)

    Write a sentence in English which explains the equality
    \begin{equation*} \int_4^{10} M(h)\,dh = 8000 \end{equation*}
    .
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Write an integral expression which gives the total amount of rainwater in cubic feet, in Avery’s backyard at 7:00 a.m.
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Write an expression which gives the average amount of rainwater, in cubic feet, in Avery’s backyard between 6:00 a.m. and 9:00 a.m.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Estimate the area under the curve \(f(x) = \frac{1}{x}\) from \(x=1\) to \(x=5\) using four rectangles.
    🔗

    (a)

    Use right endpoints. Sketch the graph and the rectangles.
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Repeat the estimation using left endpoints.
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Explain why one estimate is an overestimate and the other is an underestimate.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    The limit
    \begin{equation*} \lim_{n \to \infty} \sum_{i=1}^n \left(1 + \frac{3i}{n}\right)\frac{3}{n} \end{equation*}
    is the limit of a right Riemann sum for a definite integral \(\int_a^b f(x)\,dx\) of some function \(f\) over some interval \([a,b]\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    Identify \(a\text{,}\) \(b\text{,}\) and \(f(x)\text{,}\) and write the corresponding definite integral.
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Evaluate the integral using the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    Consider the piecewise-defined function
    \begin{equation*} f(x) = \begin{cases} x + 1 \amp \text{if } 0 \le x \le 3 \\ \sqrt{9 - (x-3)^2} + 1 \amp \text{if } 3 \le x \le 6 \end{cases} \end{equation*}
    The first piece is a line segment, and the second piece is a quarter-circle of radius 3 centered at \((3, 1)\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    Verify that \(f\) is continuous at \(x = 3\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Using geometry (not antiderivatives), evaluate the definite integral \(\int_0^6 f(x) \, dx\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5.

    Let \(g(x) = \int_0^{x^2} \sin(t^3) \, dt\text{.}\) Find \(g'(x)\) as a closed-form expression of \(x\text{.}\)

    Find the displacement of the particle over the interval.
